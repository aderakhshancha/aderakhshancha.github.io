The Utah Jazz are a professional basketball team in the National Basketball Association (NBA), based in Salt Lake City, Utah. The team was originally founded in 1974 as the New Orleans Jazz before relocating to Utah in 1979, where they kept the Jazz name despite Utah not being particularly associated with jazz music. The team plays its home games at the Delta Center and competes in the Western Conference as part of the Northwest Division. Throughout its history, the Utah Jazz have become known for their strong teamwork, passionate fan base, and competitive basketball. Some of the most famous players in franchise history include Karl Malone and John Stockton, who formed one of the greatest duos in NBA history during the 1980s and 1990s. Under their leadership, the Jazz reached the NBA Finals in 1997 and 1998, although they lost both championship series to Michael Jordan and the Chicago Bulls. The franchise has also featured talented players such as Deron Williams, Donovan Mitchell, Rudy Gobert, and Lauri Markkanen. The Utah Jazz are recognized for their purple, gold, and white team colors, their mountain-inspired logo designs, and their loyal supporters. Even through rebuilding periods and changes in players and coaching staff, the Jazz continue to work toward becoming a championship-winning organization and remain an important part of Utah's sports culture.
